This document is the starting bible. It is written so a small studio or contractor team can open it and begin pre-production: art bible, unit list, economy, modes, campaign beats, and a voice-acting slang pack including adult (18+) military profanity per dialect. Numbers are stylized for game balance, then anchored to publicly reported inventories so each army feels like itself.
1. Vision &amp; Elevator Pitch
Elevator pitch (one sentence): Command an Arab army whose tanks, jets, and curses are the ones that army actually fields — fight the AI or other players across desert, city, and coast in a colorful early-2000s 3D RTS that plays like Red Alert 3 with tighter cameras, better lighting, and a fully Arabic soul.
What the player feels after 20 minutes: I am not commanding generic ‘Middle East faction #3’. I am Egypt with Abrams and Rafales and Sa‘ka, or Saudi with F-15s and Patriots, or UAE with Leclercs and HIMARS, or Algeria with T-90s and S-400s. When a conscript screams at a burning BMP, he screams in his dialect, and he does not sound like a documentary narrator.
1.1 Design pillars
Identity first. Every faction is a real Arab armed force, compressed into a fun RTS kit. Equipment lists start from open-source inventories (IISS-style / public reporting), then get game-balanced.
RA3 energy, not milsim. Units are readable silhouettes. Abilities are flashy. Superweapons exist. Humour and swagger live in the voice lines. Combat is still deadly.
Dialect is a system. Voice packs are not one ‘Arabic’ track. Egypt, Gulf, and Maghreb sound like different countries. Adult language is part of the simulation, toggleable.
Play with anyone. Skirmish vs AI, co-op vs AI, 1v1 through 4v4 versus, spectator. Always-online optional; LAN/offline skirmish must work.
Readable early-2000s 3D. Think C&amp;C Generals / Red Alert 3 polycounts and colour, with modern PBR-lite lighting, dust, heat haze, and destruction. Not Unreal 5 photoreal. Not voxel. Not 2D.
1.2 What this is not
Not a propaganda tool and not a documentary. Campaigns use a fictional 2028–2030 crisis so no living head of state is a character.
Not a full flight sim or Steel Battalion. Aircraft are RTS aircraft: short sorties, visible, recallable.
Not 12 factions at launch. Four launch armies. More (Jordan, Morocco, Iraq, Qatar…) as DLC / season pass once the loop is fun.
Not realistic casualty counts. Units are squads and vehicles, not 440,000-man orders of battle.

2. Art, Camera &amp; Technical Direction
2.1 Visual target
Look target: ‘Red Alert 3 if it shipped in 2012 and had a good lighting artist.’ Units 2–8k triangles. Buildings 3–12k. Maps use large tiled desert/urban kits. Strong colour keys per faction so you never confuse an Egyptian olive drab Abrams with a Saudi desert tan Abrams.
Egypt: olive + sand + red-black-white flag trim. Nile green UI.
Saudi: desert tan + green flag and gold. Dark green aircraft.
UAE: sand + red/black/green/white. Clean EDGE-style grey on elite units.
Algeria: green-white-red, Russian green armour, desert camo overlays.
VFX language: dust rooster-tails, heat shimmer, tracer, Kornet/TOW smoke trails, HIMARS arcs, TOS-1 thermobaric bloom (orange-white, short, readable), Patriot/S-300 launch plumes. No grey sludge. Hits must pop.
2.2 Camera &amp; control
Classic RTS: free tilt 35–65°, zoom from ‘tabletop army’ to ‘single tank close-up’.
WASD + edge pan + minimap jump. Control groups 0–9. Classic C&amp;C sidebar + dual-ability bar on selected unit.
Every unit has ONE active ability (or two fire modes). No 8-button hero kits except named heroes.
Target platform at launch: PC (Windows). 1080p medium = GTX 1060 / RX 580 class. 1440p high = RTX 3060 class. Later console/cloud is out of scope for v1.
2.3 Suggested stack (for the developer, not locked)
Engine: Unreal Engine 5 with Nanite OFF for units (hand-authored LODs) OR Unity HDRP. Prefer Unity if the team is small and 3D RTS tooling already exists. Either works.
Netcode: lockstep deterministic for competitive 1v1/2v2; dedicated server + rollback-lite for 3v3/4v4 if lockstep desync risk is high. Spectate from replay file.
Voice: full WAV/OGG banks per faction × language pack. English UI default + full Arabic UI. Voice is always native dialect regardless of UI language.
Mod hook later: JSON unit stats, not required for v1.
3. Core Gameplay Loop
Match length target: 12–25 minutes ladder, 20–40 minutes co-op mission. Best-of style campaign missions 15–30.
3.1 Economy
Two resources: CREDITS (harvested from Oil Fields / Supply Docks) and POWER (from plants). A third soft resource: FUEL (affects aircraft sortie time and long-range MLRS; regenerates at depots).
Oil Field: captureable map resource. Needs a Harvester / Fuel Truck loop. Destroying an enemy field denies income.
Supply Dock / Port: naval maps have tanker berths.
No Tiberium. No magic ore crystals. The fantasy is oil, docks, and depots.
Saudi unique: ‘Aramco Bonus’ — Oil Fields they own generate +15% credits. UAE unique: ‘Sovereign Fund’ — first 8 minutes +10% build speed on elite buildings. Egypt unique: ‘Strategic Depth’ — Construction Yard rebuilds 20% cheaper once. Algeria unique: ‘State Industry’ — War Factory units 8% cheaper.
3.2 Base building
Classic MCV → Construction Yard. Buildings place in a radius. Pack/unpack MCV allowed once (anti-turtle but not Empire-of-Rising-Sun free build anywhere).
Power Plant → Barracks → War Factory → Airfield / Helipad → Naval Yard (water maps) → Tech / Command → Superweapon.
Defenses: bunker / HMG nest, ATGM tower, AA gun, SAM battery. Faction-flavoured (see matrices).
Walls exist but are expensive. Desert maps reward manoeuvre over fortress.

Hero — COLONEL TAREK ‘EL-NISR’ FAHMY: Sa‘ka officer. Cloak when still. Can mark a vehicle for +30% damage from all Egyptian AT for 8 seconds. Voice: gravel Cairene, dark humour.
Superweapon — OCTOBER STRIKE: a mixed package (Rafale + F-16 + one ballistic-style conventional strike on a large radius). Long charge. Interceptable by high-tier SAM if the player invested.
Ultimate (late) — MISTRAL OVER THE BEACH: on water-capable maps, a scripted helicopter-carrier arrival that drops two waves of vehicles. On land maps it becomes a heavy airlift of two MBTs + IFVs.

4.2 Saudi Arabia — القوات البرية الملكية / RSAF
Doctrine: Pay more, get better. Fewer tanks than Egypt, better air force, layered Patriot/Hawk flavour AA. Economy leans on oil. Open desert maps are their home. They punish players who skip AA.
Unique mechanic — RSAF TASKING: instead of building every aircraft at the airfield, the player spends a ‘sortie point’ (regenerates) to call an F-15 strike or Typhoon CAP from off-map. Visible inbound. Can be intercepted. This keeps the sky feeling like a kingdom-scale air force without parking 20 jets on a pad.
Hero — PRINCE BANDAR CALLSIGN ‘SAQR’: F-15SA ace that can land and become a (vulnerable) ground hero or stay in air. Air mode is a strong fighter. Ground mode is a laser-designator that buffs incoming RSAF strikes.
Superweapon — GREEN CRESCENT: a large Patriot-supported stand-off strike (F-15SA + Storm Shadow-flavour / long-range). Alternative late-game: DF-3 conventional variant as a theatre ballistic that can be shot down by THAAD-equivalent (UAE) or S-400 (Algeria).

5. Balance Philosophy (for the designer on the team)
Rock-paper-scissors must be teachable in one skirmish: tanks beat infantry in open, ATGM beat tanks if they see them, artillery beats ATGM if they are spotted, air beats artillery if AA is missing, SAM beats air if the bubble is up.
Do not make Saudi ‘just better Egypt’. Egypt wins on map control and numbers. Saudi wins on air and quality. UAE wins on timing attacks. Algeria wins on the late wall and the bubble.
Naval is a mode, not a requirement. 40% of ladder maps have meaningful water. 60% are land.
No nuclear weapons. Conventional only. Superweapons are scary, not campaign-enders unless the opponent ignored them.
Women exist in UAE and (optionally) other factions as operators and the UAE hero. Do not make it a lecture. Just cast them.
Israel, Iran, USA, Russia, France are NOT playable in v1. They can appear as campaign NPCs / off-map support if a mission needs it, never as a third-team stomper that steals the fantasy.
6. Modes
6.1 Skirmish vs AI
Difficulties: Recruit / Officer / Staff / Marshal. Marshal cheats on economy 10% and reacts faster, does not spawn free units.
AI personalities per faction: Egypt ‘Front’ (attacks on a broad line), Saudi ‘Sky’ (air + artillery), UAE ‘Raid’ (flank + HIMARS), Algeria ‘Fortress’ (turtle then TOS).
Map settings: resources high/med/low, random crates on/off, superweapons on/off, revealed map on/off.
6.2 Co-op vs AI
2–4 humans share a side against 2–4 AI. Shared or separate bases (playlist both).
Co-op campaign: each story mission supports 2 players with split objectives (one holds the ridge, one hits the port).
Friendly fire off by default in co-op, on in versus.
6.3 Versus (online)
1v1, 2v2, 3v3, 4v4. Ranked 1v1 and 2v2 only at launch.
Unranked custom: pick maps, modifiers (no air, no SW, sudden death, low power).
Observers / replay. Chat with mute. Ping system.
Anti-cheat: server-authoritative commands + replay hash. Keep it simple in v1.
Regions: EMEA first (the audience). NA/SEA later.
6.4 Tutorial &amp; Army School
10-minute boot camp (move, build, counter).
Faction primers: 1 short mission each that forces you to use the unique mechanic.
Encyclopaedia: unit vs unit chart, like RA3.
7. Campaign Script Outline
Tone: geopolitical pulp, not news. Year 2029. A fictional private military-intelligence network called AL-MIZAN (‘The Balance’) has bought officers, jammed satellites, and staged false-flag attacks to set Arab armies against each other while they seize oil nodes and ports. Each campaign is that army’s version of the same month. Players who finish two campaigns unlock the co-op finale.
No living politician is named. Presidents and kings are ‘the Command’, ‘the Palace’, ‘the Council’.
7.1 Structure
4 campaigns × 7 missions = 28. Plus 1 co-op finale (2 missions).
Mission types mix: assault, defend, raid, naval landing, city clear, escort, superweapon race.
Between missions: a simple situation room with a map of North Africa + Arabian Peninsula. Briefing is VO + text. Language: player’s UI language; radio in dialect.
7.2 Egypt campaign — ‘THE RIVER HOLDS’
Logline: After a drone swarm hits a Sinai radar and a false recording implicates a Gulf partner, Cairo must secure the Canal, the Delta, and a Red Sea port before Al-Mizan turns the country into a corridor.
E1 Sinai Glass: tutorial-plus. Hold two passes against militia-flagged armour (Al-Mizan proxies using mixed surplus). Teach Kornet + M60 + one F-16.
E2 Night on the Canal: defend / repair Suez infrastructure. Naval intro. Missile boats vs dummy freighters that spawn commandos.
E3 Sa‘ka in the Alleys: urban Cairo-adjacent. Garrison game. Hero intro. No tanks in the old city until the player blows a wall.
E4 Wings over Beni Suef: airfield attacked. Learn F-16 vs MiG-29M mix vs incoming strike packages.
E5 Mistral to the Islands: amphibious. Player uses the carrier fantasy to take a Red Sea island used as a missile farm.
E6 The False Recording: raid Al-Mizan server farm in the desert. Stealth optional. If detected, full conventional.
E7 October, Again: defend the capital approaches against a coalition-sized proxy assault. Superweapon unlock. Ending: Egypt holds, but Al-Mizan relocates west.
7.3 Saudi campaign — ‘GREEN BANNER’
Logline: Oil nodes in the Eastern Province are sabotaged. The sky is full of unexplained drones. Riyadh answers with the thing it bought the most of: aircraft.
S1 Pump Station 6: defend an oil field. Teach harvester protection + M-ATV reaction.
S2 Empty Quarter Ghosts: recon in vast desert. Bradley + Apache. Ambush design.
S3 Patriot Weather: first mass cruise-missile attack. Player must keep Patriot nodes alive. Teach RSAF Tasking.
S4 The Causeway: land-naval hybrid. Teach frigate + F-15.
S5 Palace Guard: urban Riyadh-adjacent, constrained ROE (do not flatten the district). Precision only.
S6 Saqr Over the Ridge: hero mission. Player is mostly the ace plus a small ground screen.
S7 Banner Unbroken: combined defence of the energy spine. Ending: Saudi sky is clean; Al-Mizan has a port in the Maghreb.
7.4 UAE campaign — ‘FALCON PROTOCOL’
Logline: A precision war. Someone is using the Emirates’ own logistics software against them. The Presidential Guard does not do meat waves.
U1 Port Khalifa Shadow: counter-sabotage. SF + Nimr. Tight timer.
U2 Leclerc at Dawn: first armour fight in open sabkha. Teach autoloader micro.
U3 HIMARS Math: player is not allowed a tank mass. Win with rockets, drones-as-vision, and two jets.
U4 Island Chain: naval + air. Corvettes and Block 60s.
U5 The Hotel: urban hostage-adjacent (extract a defector). Civilian collateral fails the mission.
U6 EDGE Broken: enemy has cloned UAE munitions. Fight a mirror kit. Identity crisis mission.
U7 Protocol Complete: simultaneous defence of two nodes. Co-op recommended. Ending: Al-Mizan unmasked as a board, not a country.
7.5 Algeria campaign — ‘THE TELL AND THE ERG’
Logline: Mountain and desert. A foreign-flagged ‘stabilisation force’ (Al-Mizan) tries to peel the south. Algiers answers with T-90s and a sky that kills aircraft.
A1 Djebel Ambush: teach elevation + Kornet + cheap T-72 screen.
A2 Highway of Rust: defend a long road with TOS-1 ambush points.
A3 Bubble: first S-300 mission. Player must choose where the umbrella lives.
A4 Oran Night: naval + Kilo sub. Club-S shot as a set-piece.
A5 Terminator Escort: a column of T-90s must cross open ground. BMPT is the star.
A6 Smerch Conversation: counter-battery duel vs UAE-flavour HIMARS proxies.
A7 Tell Atlas: final conventional battle. Ending: Al-Mizan burns; the four commands realise they were pointed at each other.
7.6 Finale (unlock, 2 players recommended) — ‘THE BALANCE BREAKS’

8. Voice, Dialect &amp; 18+ Slang Bible
This section is for the writer, the voice director, and the audio lead. Adult language is a design feature of a war game, not graffiti on the script. It is how conscripts actually talk when a tank is on fire. It is also how officers talk when they think the net is closed.
8.1 Rules
Three layers per faction: COMMAND (almost clean, MSA or formal dialect), OFFICER (sharp, some profanity), TROOP (full slang, 18+).
A settings slider: Family / Military / Uncut. Family replaces 18+ lines with the clean take we also record. Default on store pages: Military. Uncut is an option in-game.
Do not use sectarian slurs. Do not use slurs against a real ethnic or religious group. Do not sexualise children. Adult sexual insults aimed at an enemy combatant are allowed in Uncut because that is how this language is actually used in anger.
Do not mock the Qur’an, the prayer, or the shahada. Soldiers can swear by God in the ordinary way (‘wallah’, ‘yil‘an’). Keep the sacred out of the punchline.
Record more lines than you think. RTS units repeat. We need 8–12 selects, 6–10 moves, 6–10 attacks, 4 deaths, 3 under-fire, 2 idle jokes, 2 victory.
English UI can stay English. Combat VO stays dialect even if the player is in English UI. Subtitles optional, off by default for Uncut.
